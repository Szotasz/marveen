**OUR OWN SYSTEMS' TEST EMAILS LOOK LIKE REAL CUSTOMER EVENTS (2026-08-17, measured).** The bot exclusion was built for THIRD-PARTY notifications (code host, deploy provider, CI). There is another class that none of the filters catches: your own product's transactional emails, which WE OURSELVES triggered with a test a few hours ago. In the mailbox these look true to life, because they are: real password-reset and real invitation emails, with a real timestamp, from our own domain. That morning four of these sat in the inbox from an overnight end-to-end measurement, and if they get into the morning kickoff, the owner sees a new sign-up and a password problem where there is only the trace of our own QA.
**PROCEDURE:** when going through the emails, ask whether WE triggered the given email in the past few hours (test account, plus-addressed address, our own `noreply@` sender from our own domain, an invitation and a password email arriving as a pair). If yes, it is not news but a by-product, and it is left out. The suspicion is cheap to check: the related kanban card contains the test addresses and the time of the measurement. Related: `feedback_third_party_test_residue`.

**THE FORMAT: MarkdownV2 is risky here, and there is a proven third way.** This genre (long, multi-section, full of periods, hyphens and times) has died several times with manual escaping. If you write the text BY HAND into the send call, go with plain text, and let emoji and capitals make the section titles. If MarkdownV2 is still needed, use MACHINE escaping (every reserved character without exception, swapped back from the placeholder character pair that stands in for bold), and VALIDATE before sending: zero unescaped reserved characters, an even number of asterisks. Details: `telegram-markdownv2-escape`.

**MOST OF THE NEWS SEARCH IS AGGREGATOR BLOGS, AND THAT IS NOT A SOURCE (2026-08-09, measured).** The first page of an "AI news <date>" search is typically a list of SEO blogs, full of concrete-looking model names and version numbers (that day: a supposed GPT version and two other "releases"), which come from a SINGLE aggregator, with no primary announcement. Stating these in the morning kickoff is exactly the error that `feedback_verify_facts_live_source` forbids: the owner reads it as fact and passes it on.
**PROCEDURE:** take in only an item where the news has a CONCRETE, verifiable anchor (date + measurable data + a named publisher), and leave the rest out. At the end of the section write ONE line about where the material came from, and what you left out as unverified. NEVER state a model name, version or price from an aggregator blog.

**AND THE PROVENANCE LINE IS MANDATORY EVEN IF YOU LEFT NOTHING OUT (2026-08-22, my own omission, measured).** The paragraph above is about LEAVING THINGS OUT, so if the search gives three usable-looking news items and you drop none of them, the closing line naturally drops out: this is exactly what happened to me. The consequence is not that a line is missing, but that **all three items look EQUALLY certain**: the reader cannot tell the item backed by a named publisher from the third one that came from an aggregator. That day the first two (Bloomberg/CNBC/TechCrunch, and the vendor's own announcement page) held up afterwards, the third (an industry ratio) could NOT be traced back to a primary source, and the owner passes on exactly this kind of number in talks.
**PROCEDURE: the closing line is a MANDATORY part of the section, not an appendix to leaving things out.** Its form: where the material came from, which item HAS a named publisher, and which one does NOT. If an item has none, mark that readably in the morning kickoff, not only in the closing line.
**AND THE ANCHOR SEARCH IS CHEAP IF IT RUNS ON THE RISKY ITEM:** you do not need to look up every news item, only the one that contains a NUMBER or a PRODUCT CLAIM (revenue, ratio, version, a feature tied to a date). One targeted search with the publisher's name settles it, and brings the better content along too: in my case the vendor's own page gave the mechanism detail that turned the news item into a usable video topic.

**THE "FACT DATA" SECTIONS OF THE DREAM.md ARE NOT ALL EQUALLY DURABLE EITHER (2026-08-22, measured).** The Dream rule above says that the Top-3 must be measured again, while the memory health and skill fleet sections "can be taken over unchanged, they are fact data". This sentence hides a difference: the measured RATIOS (vectorization, duplicate count) really do hold until morning, but among the COUNTS, the ones that OUR OWN NIGHT WORK changes are stale by dawn. In my case the skill count: the Dream wrote 227 at 02:07, by morning it was 229, because the night rounds had created two skills.
**PROCEDURE: every number that we ourselves can move within two hours (skill count, card count, open PRs), measure again with one command before sending;** take over the ratios that come from an external measurement. A wrong number in the "fact data" section is worse than a missing one: the reader trusts it exactly where they do not check it.
